Proposal: a Decide session over the Desk

The Desk stays the overview. A prominent Decide · 6 button on the Desk (also a shortcut, a command palette action and a Go menu item) opens a full-window session over the same queue: one decision at a time, quick ones first, then the ones that need the Stage or a Preview. When you finish you are back on the Desk. Click into the window and play it with the keys, or use the buttons. Switch the layout to compare the alternatives.

One card per kind of decision

The same layout every time: what is asked, the choices with what each gains and costs, the recommendation with its reason, then the actions. This or that (side by side), pick one (1 to 4), yes or no (a plan, a rewrite), answer (suggested answers as chips), and "needs a sitting" for Proposals and verifying, which open the Stage or a Preview.

Safe without sheets

A confirmation sheet on every accept would kill the flow. Instead each decision waits ten seconds before Hatch acts, with Undo in the toast, and only then does an agent start. Each card says what accepting starts. Every move still goes through HatchStore.move; nothing new is stored.

Fun, but quiet

A time estimate at the start, one card at a time with a progress row, cards that slide away with a light trackpad tap, and a short summary at the end: how many you cleared, how often you agreed with the recommendation, how many agents started. Overruling a kind of recommendation often tells Hatch its recommendations need work. Reduce Motion turns the motion off.

Alternatives I considered

Recommended

A · Focus card

One card in the middle of a calm window, progress at the top. Nothing else competes, every kind of card fits, and the keys are the same everywhere.

B · Card stack

The next cards peek out behind, so you see the pile shrink. More game-like, but the stack suggests swiping yes or no, which does not fit "pick one of four".

C · Queue and card

The queue on the left, the card on the right. Closest to today's Desk and easy to jump around, but it is the Desk again with a bigger pane, and the list pulls your eye away from the decision.
