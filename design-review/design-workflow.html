Hatch Workflow

You write a prompt. Iris works out what it is, asks only what she cannot guess, and sends it down the right path. Agents and Hatch do the rest, and Hatch comes to you only when it really needs you: a real choice, something you can see, or a question only you can answer.

This page covers the whole journey for every kind of work: questions, visual changes, architecture and structure, bugs, slowness, small changes, chores and big multi-part work. It sets out what the code does today and where it breaks, and how each stage should work. Questions for you sit in the stage they belong to, each with my recommendation (★) and the reason.

2 · Where Hatch needs you

The complete list of times Hatch stops for you

Every item below arrives the same way: one card in Decide, a notification, the count on the Dock and in the menu bar, and the banner on the ticket. If a stop is not on this list, the proposed workflow should not stop for it.

Iris cannot guessWhat is wrong, which screen, what “better” means. At most three questions at a time, each with suggested answers and her best guess already picked.
Something conflictsA ticket that would undo an earlier decision, a probable duplicate she is not sure of, or a split of one prompt into several tickets.
A real choiceQuestion options, a web draft, a Swift Stage, a written proposal, or findings with more than one sensible fix.
A large planMore files than the project's limit, or a protected area. A plain Bug no longer waits for approval on its own.
An agent is stuckA question only you can answer while it works. It pauses there and carries on where it stopped once you answer.
Something you can seeVerify in a Preview with a short checklist, or check before and after numbers for slowness.
Hatch cannot fix it itselfA merge conflict between two tickets, CI failing twice, or an agent that stopped twice even on a stronger model.

What no longer needs you

Choosing the type, the title, the area, the priority and the links. Accepting Iris's rewrite. Submitting a draft. Approving every Bug's plan. Picking tickets for a Preview. Pressing Merge on a clean plan. Promoting to dev on green CI. Closing Merged tickets. Verifying chores and internal restructuring that tests and CI cover. Iris does these and lists them in the ticket's history and a daily digest, and each one can be changed or undone.

7 · Then

Build order

  1. Repair what is broken today (G7, G11, G21–G26). These are bugs under any answer: Merged reaching Done, file claims freed when work is handed in, your Resume on Blocked, resumed tickets being picked up again, an agent's question resuming the work instead of restarting it, Iris starting for tickets made by the CLI or from GitHub, Preview opening the app it built, and the draft's files kept in Hatch. I can start these before you answer.
  2. Prompt in, Iris routes (stages 1–4): the one-field composer and quick capture everywhere, Iris's new result (path, area, priority, links, split, confidence), applying what she is sure of, her questions in rounds, the re-check after your answers, the digest.
  3. The paths and what you judge (stages 5–7): Question follow-ups, written proposals, investigations with findings and numbers, the common exits on Your call, type changes during work.
  4. Visual: draft first (stage 6 and 7): draft offers on Proposals, Accept draft, drafts on Decide cards.
  5. The Swift Stage (stage 8): the round template, Hatch builds and checks it at hatch offer, one Stage per Proposal, Reload, and a live run with Hatch.
  6. Checks, verify and land (stages 9–12): verification per path, Iris's checklists, merge on Looks right, push and pull request, CI to Done, promote.
  7. Seven real runs: the seven examples above on a throwaway database with real agents, then STATUS.md says what passed and what did not.

Every step ends with swift test at the root and in Stage, tools/smoke.sh, the Xcode build, and a look at the running app. Nothing is called working until it has run.
