The menu bar panel

What is off in today's panel, what it should show, four ways to style it, and the icon. Switch the state above the styles to see each one busy, quiet, paused and with a sync problem. Pick what you like at the bottom and copy it for Claude.

1 · Today

Nine things that make it feel crowded

The panel mixes four looks (caption headings, a bordered button, wrapping pills and menu rows), and most of the space goes to answering questions that are cut off before you can read them.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9
  1. 1
    A dark smear over the top third. The panel's glass shows the window behind it through the header and Agents rows. System menus use a denser material, so this reads as a rendering bug. The likely cause is plain content on the clear window glass; I have not confirmed it in code.
  2. 2
    The header repeats what you just clicked. The egg and "Hatch" are the menu bar item itself. "Everything is saved" is news only when it is not true.
  3. 3
    Three lines to say nothing runs. A heading, "0 of 3" and a sentence for an empty state.
  4. 4
    The count twice. "13" in a circle and "13 tickets wait for you" next to it, then a small bordered Open Desk button that goes to the Desk, not to Decide.
  5. 5
    One ticket, two entries. #15 shows up as two separate questions with the same header.
  6. 6
    Questions cut mid-sentence. "What about the "Iris asks" bubbles feels wrong to…" and then you are asked to answer it with one click. Decide shows the whole message for this reason.
  7. 7
    Nine answer pills in wrapping rows. The orange first pill looks already chosen rather than recommended, and one stray click sends an answer with no undo.
  8. 8
    An old activity line. "#1 moved to To verify, 7h" is history with nothing to do about it.
  9. 9
    Settings… is cut off. The panel grows with the questions until it runs out of room. There is also no Quit item, which every menu bar app needs.

3 · Style

Four ways to style it

Same data in each. A and B follow the recommended contents. C keeps answering in the panel but fixes how. D borrows Control Center's tiles. Hover the rows to see how they highlight.

A

A real menu

A system menu instead of a window. Decide… carries the count as a badge and the kinds as a subtitle; running agents are items under an Agents header.

Good

  • Looks exactly like every other menu, in every macOS setting
  • Keyboard and VoiceOver for free
  • No glass or height bugs possible

Less good

  • Plain: no big count, no progress
  • Elapsed time only updates while the menu is open

.menuBarExtraStyle(.menu), Button rows with .badge, Section("Agents"), subtitles via a two-line label.

B

A calm status panel

Recommended

One block for what waits, with one prominent Decide button. Agents appear only while they work. A problem appears only when there is one. The rest are menu rows.

Good

  • The count is the first thing you see, in your colour
  • One prominent action, as DESIGN.md asks
  • Short when quiet, never taller than about 420 pt

Less good

  • Answering moves to Decide and notifications
  • Still a window, so it needs a solid material

.menuBarExtraStyle(.window) with a denser background than the clear glass, .glassProminent Decide button, rows in the existing MenuBarRowStyle.

C

One decision at a time

A small Decide: the top question with its whole message, the answers as Decide's list, Later and Answer. Agents and actions shrink to one footer line.

Good

  • Answer without opening Hatch
  • Same answer list as Decide, so nothing new to learn
  • Selecting does nothing until you press Answer

Less good

  • The heaviest option; a design question deserves the big view
  • Height changes with each question

Reuses QuestionMessage and AnswerList inside the window panel, with a fixed height and its own scroll.

D

Control Center tiles

Two tiles: Decide with the count, Agents with how many work (click it to pause). A wide tile for problems, the agents in short rows, then the actions.

Good

  • Feels at home next to Control Center in macOS 27
  • Pausing agents is one click on a tile

Less good

  • Tiles take room even when idle
  • A tile that toggles and one that opens look the same

Window panel, tiles as filled rounded rectangles (no glass on glass), Button rows below.

4 · In the menu bar

The icon

Another change in progress replaces the arch with an egg that cracks when something waits. The open question is whether the count should sit next to it.

Crack only

Recommended

Whole egg when nothing waits, cracked when something does. Calm, and the panel has the number.

Crack and count

The number beside the cracked egg, like Mail's Dock badge. Precise, but it changes width and pulls the eye all day.

Count when 5 or more

Crack for a few, the number once the pile grows, so it nags only when it matters.

Recommendation

B, the calm status panel, with the crack-only icon. It keeps the one thing the menu bar is for (what waits and a fast way to Decide) and drops what made today's panel crowded: inline answering, the empty Agents block, the activity line and the always-on save status. Answering stays one click away in Decide and in the notification.

Next, as with the Decide Lab, I can build A and B (or whichever you pick) as switchable native variants behind a Menu Bar Lab, so you judge them with real data in the real menu bar before we commit.
