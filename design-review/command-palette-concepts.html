Command palette (⌘K)

You asked for two things: make the palette look native, and make it feel like a real command palette rather than a list. Below are the problems I see today, my proposal as a working mockup, and the decisions I need from you. My recommendation is already selected in every question.

What is wrong today

How it looks

  • It is a sheet: it slides out of the toolbar, dims the window and sits in an opaque white box. Spotlight in macOS 26 and later is a floating glass panel.
  • The window is a fixed 560 × 420. Two results leave a large empty area, as in your screenshot with no project.
  • Each row is two lines, and the second line says "Command" every time. The list is twice as tall as it needs to be.

How it works

  • With nothing typed it lists the same "Go to …" pages that the sidebar and ⌘1–9 already give you. It shows nothing about your work.
  • Results are one flat list with no groups, so tickets, Spec items and commands are hard to tell apart.
  • Return is the only action. To move a ticket or copy its link you have to open it first.
  • Matching is plain "contains", and shortcuts are never shown, so the palette doesn't teach them.

Proposal

A floating Liquid Glass panel over the window, like Spotlight. It opens empty-ish and helpful, groups what it finds, and acts on what you've selected. Switch the states below; hover a row to select it.

Native look

A floating panel with the system's Liquid Glass (.glassEffect(.regular, in: .rect(cornerRadius:))), drawn over the window instead of as a sheet. The window isn't dimmed and nothing slides. The selected row is a plain accent fill, since there is no glass on glass (DESIGN rule 3). With Reduce Transparency on, the system makes it opaque.

Structure

Results come in groups (Waiting for you, Recent, Tickets, Spec, Actions, Go to), each with a small header. Rows are one line: number and title on the left, status or shortcut on the right. The panel grows with the results up to eight rows, then scrolls.

Feel

Fuzzy matching ("mc" finds Manage Connections) with the matched letters in bold. Return does the obvious thing and Tab shows everything else you can do. The footer always says what the keys do and which project you're searching.

Alternatives I considered

A sheet with a glass background

The smallest change, but it still slides out of the toolbar and dims the window. macOS draws sheet chrome around it, so the glass reads as a box inside a box.

A floating panel in the window

Recommended

Looks and behaves like Spotlight, stays in Hatch's window and uses only SwiftUI. Click outside or press Esc to close it.

A system-wide panel with a global shortcut

Like Raycast: capture a ticket from any app. Useful, but it needs an AppKit panel and a global hotkey. It's better kept for later as "Quick capture from anywhere".
